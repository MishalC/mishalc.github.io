Product and business analysis

Three pieces of analysis from 2026, each told as the problem, what I analysed, what was decided and what came of it. The numbers are counts from my own records.

Gap analysis before a mobile launch

Problem
A mobile app was due to launch on 15 April 2026, and its requirements document had not been checked against what the build and the tests needed.
Analysis
I analysed the document ahead of the launch and found 14 gaps, 4 of them critical.
Decision
Each gap became work: a ticket breakdown for engineering, test cases for QA and a delivery plan for the launch.
Outcome
The breakdown, the test cases and the plan were ready before the 15 April launch.

Backlog audit

Problem
In September 2026 the backlog held 576 tickets with an owner and a priority, across four teams, and it was unclear which were still worth keeping.
Analysis
An AI-assisted audit flagged 131: 76 to close and 55 to route to a lead. A second AI pass tried to disprove the first. It challenged 30 keep verdicts and overturned none.
Decision
I approved every close myself. Where a reversible close-or-keep call was stuck, I made it and recorded why. My calls were on the backlog, not on product strategy, which stayed with the product owners.
Outcome
95 tickets closed, each with the reason recorded.

Prototypes instead of long documents

Problem
Developers found it hard to work out what to build from long PRDs and large documents.
Analysis
A short brief per idea, then a clickable prototype built with an AI coding agent, reviewed with product and design before any engineering work.
Decision
Ideas went to engineering as a prototype plus tickets, not as a document alone.
Outcome
15 interactive prototypes in 2026. Seven are on this site as clickable copies, with names and data replaced.

Decision record: the MVP cut

Situation
In October 2026 an itinerary redesign had to be cut to an MVP, and the design was too large to decide on as one piece.
Options
Decide on the whole redesign at once, or split it into its items and decide item by item.
Call
I split the redesign into its items, so the cut could be decided one item at a time.
Result
The itemised list became the basis for the MVP cut decision.

Decision record: telling the reporter

Situation
Releases reached production on most weekdays, about 36 tickets a weekday. People who reported bugs had no seat in the tracker, so they could not see when a fix went out.
Options
Give every reporter a tracker seat, ask engineers to reply by hand, or have the release automation reply where the report was made.
Call
The automation replies in the reporter's own thread when their issue reaches production, found from the link on the issue.
Result
Reporters hear back without a tracker seat and without chasing. Live from July 2026.
